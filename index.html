The law in sixty seconds

What is Martyn's Law?

The Terrorism (Protection of Premises) Act 2025 — named for Martyn Hett, one of the 22 people killed at Manchester Arena in 2017. It asks venues where 200 or more people may gather to be prepared to protect them. Not fortified. Prepared.

April 2025

Royal Assent

The Act becomes law, with at least 24 months before duties begin.

April 2026

Statutory guidance published

The Home Office sets out exactly what venues must have in place.

Before you buy from anyone — including us

Martyn's Law has attracted sharks. Five claims that should make you walk away from any provider:

"Compliance certificate"

No certificate exists in law. Nobody can certify you compliant — not us, not anyone. Training records? Useful. Certificates of compliance? Fiction.

"Official" or "Government-approved"

The Home Office and SIA endorse no product or provider. Any provider implying otherwise is misleading you.

"You need CCTV, barriers or guards"

Not at standard tier. The Government's own MythBuster says procedures, not equipment.

Wrong numbers

"Applies from 100 capacity", "Tier 1" — the real thresholds are 200 (standard) and 800 (enhanced). Sellers who get the basics wrong shouldn't write your procedures.

Countdown-timer panic

Spring 2027 is real; the panic isn't. The SIA has said it will be supportive and proportionate. Anyone selling fear is selling for themselves.

Straight answers

Questions volunteers actually ask

Do we need CCTV, barriers or security staff?
No. Standard-tier duties are procedures, not equipment. The Government's own guidance says most venues need no physical works at all — and warns against providers who claim otherwise.
We're a small church — surely this doesn't apply to us?
Run the free check above. If fewer than 200 people could reasonably be present at your busiest occasion (counting helpers), you're out of scope — and we'll give you a letter for your records saying so. Places of worship that are in scope stay in the lighter standard tier whatever their size.
Is documentation legally required?
Honestly: at standard tier, the Act requires procedures to exist and work — it doesn't mandate paperwork. But written procedures are how you train volunteers, brief hirers, survive committee turnover and answer the regulator confidently. That's what the pack gives you.
Can't we just use our fire evacuation plan?
It's a foundation, not a substitute. In a terrorism scenario the safest direction can invert — sometimes the right move is to stay in, or to avoid the usual exit. The four procedures cover what fire plans don't.
What happens if we do nothing?
From Spring 2027 the SIA can require compliance and issue penalties of up to £10,000 for standard-tier venues, with daily penalties for continuing breaches. The regulator has said it will be supportive and proportionate — especially with venues that have made a start.
Do we get a compliance certificate?
No — because no such certificate exists in law, and we won't invent one. What the Complete Kit gives you is what the guidance actually values: written procedures, and Briefing Completion Records showing each volunteer was trained, filed in your evidence log. If another provider offers you a "compliance certificate", show them the door.
Is this endorsed by the Home Office or SIA?
No — and be wary of anyone implying they are. The Government endorses no third-party product, including ours. Everything in the pack is mapped, clause by clause, to the published statutory guidance, which is free to read on GOV.UK. We save you the drafting time; the duty stays yours.
