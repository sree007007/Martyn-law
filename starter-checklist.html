The starter checklist

Your venue is in scope at standard tier — the lighter set of duties. That means seven things, and no more than seven. Work down this list in order; each one builds on the last. Three of them cost you nothing but time, and all seven can be done without buying anything from anyone — the official guidance is free on GOV.UK. What a pack buys you is the drafting, not permission.

1

Write down the number, and how you got it

The peak number reasonably expected at the same time at your busiest realistic occasion — counting visitors, staff, volunteers and performers. Note where the figure came from (seating count, fire risk assessment, last year's carol service). This one sentence is the foundation of everything below, and the first thing anyone will ask you about.

Free
2

Name the responsible person, and minute it

It is whoever controls the premises — usually the body rather than an individual: the PCC, the trustees, the parish council. Record the decision in the minutes so it survives a change of committee. The duty cannot be delegated away to a hirer, a caretaker or a consultant.

Free
3

Get your notification details ready

You will need to notify the Security Industry Authority once its portal opens ahead of commencement. Put the details in one place now — premises name and address, who the responsible person is, and a contact email that will still be monitored in two years' time. It takes ten minutes when you already have the answers.

Free
4

Write the four procedures

Evacuation — getting people out, and where they gather. Invacuation — bringing people in and securing them, when out is the more dangerous direction. Lockdown — which doors lock, who has the key, how you stop people entering. Communication — how you raise the alarm among yourselves without causing panic, and who calls 999. They have to be proportionate to your building, and they have to be capable of being carried out by the people actually on duty — not by staff you don't have.

The drafting is what a pack saves you
5

Make sure your people know them

This is an explicit ask in the Act, not an optional extra: staff and volunteers must be made aware of the procedures so they can carry them out. A twenty-minute briefing at a meeting you were having anyway is enough. Write down who attended and when — that record is your evidence, and it is worth more than any certificate.

6

Put the review in the diary

Once a year, walk round and check the plan still fits the building — and sooner if something material changes. Twenty minutes. Often the honest answer is "nothing has changed", and recording that is a complete review.

Free
7

Talk to anyone you share the building with

Shared premises and regular hirers: agree who leads in an emergency and make sure the procedures line up rather than contradict each other. Give hirers a single page they can act on — most of them will never have thought about it.

What is not on the list

CCTV · barriers · bag searches · security staff · a risk assessment submitted to anyone · a paid consultant · a course certificate. None of these are standard-tier requirements. If a provider is selling you one of them as "required by Martyn's Law", ask which of the seven it satisfies — and watch what happens.

You can do all of this yourself, for nothing. The statutory guidance is free at GOV.UK, and free ACT awareness training is available at ProtectUK.
