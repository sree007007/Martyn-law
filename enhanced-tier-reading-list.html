You need more than a pack

Why we are sending you away

Your check came out at 800 or more people at a realistic busiest occasion, and your venue is not a place of worship. That puts you in the enhanced tier, which carries materially heavier duties than the standard tier our documents are written for.

We could sell you our pack anyway. It would be a foundation and it would not be sufficient, and you would find that out at the worst possible moment. So here is the reading list instead, free, with nothing to buy from us at the end of it.

One thing worth checking first: places of worship remain in the standard tier whatever their capacity. If your venue is a church, chapel or other place of worship, run the check again with that selected — the answer changes, and the lighter duties apply to you.

What the enhanced tier adds

Everything the standard tier asks for still applies. On top of it, and stated here in outline only — the guidance is the authority, not this sheet:

1

Public protection measures, not just procedures

Standard tier asks you to have plans. Enhanced tier also asks you to consider measures that reduce vulnerability and the risk of physical harm — monitoring, physical safety and security, and how the premises are secured — so far as is reasonably practicable.

2

A designated senior individual

Where the responsible person is a body rather than a person, a named senior individual carries responsibility for the duties being met. This is a real accountability, and worth agreeing at board level rather than delegating downwards.

3

A documented assessment, provided to the regulator

Enhanced-tier premises must document how the measures and procedures reduce risk, and provide that document to the Security Industry Authority. This is the point people most often get wrong about the standard tier — at standard tier there is no document to submit to anyone. At enhanced tier there is.

4

A different penalty regime

Standard-tier penalties reach up to £10,000. Enhanced-tier penalties are of a different order — reported as up to £18 million or 5% of qualifying worldwide revenue. Check the current figures and the definition of qualifying worldwide revenue in the guidance before you quote them to your board.

What to read, in this order

The statutory guidance — the enhanced-tier chapters

Published by the Home Office and free. Read the enhanced-tier sections in full rather than a provider's summary of them; summaries are where the mis-selling starts.

Search GOV.UK for: Terrorism (Protection of Premises) Act 2025 statutory guidance

The Act itself, Part 1 and Schedule 1

Worth an hour if you are the designated senior individual. Schedule 1 is where qualifying premises and uses are defined, and it settles most arguments about whether you are in scope at all.

legislation.gov.uk — Terrorism (Protection of Premises) Act 2025

The regulator's own material

The Security Industry Authority is the regulator, and has said its approach will be supportive and proportionate. Its enforcement guidance and the notification process are the practical detail you will actually be judged against.

gov.uk/government/organisations/security-industry-authority

ProtectUK, for the training side

Free ACT awareness training and practical counter-terrorism material for staff. Genuinely free, genuinely official, and it covers the awareness element without a purchase.

protectuk.police.uk

If you bring in a specialist, ask these

  • Which specific requirement of the Act does each thing you are proposing satisfy? Anything that cannot be mapped to one is a want, not a duty.
  • What are you assuming our peak occupancy is, and where did that number come from?
  • What will we still have to do ourselves after you have finished?
  • What does the documented assessment look like, and who signs it?
  • What happens at renewal — is there an annual fee, and what does it actually buy?
Two warnings that apply at any tier. Nobody can sell you a "compliance certificate" — no such thing exists in this law.
