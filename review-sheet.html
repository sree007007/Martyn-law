The 20-minute annual review

Martyn's Law asks standard-tier venues to keep their procedures current — that's requirement 6 of the seven. It doesn't ask for a report, a submission or an inspection. It asks you to look again, once a year, and change anything that no longer fits the building. Walk round with this sheet, tick as you go, and file it with your fire log. If nothing has changed, that is a perfectly good result — write "no changes" and sign it.

Has anything changed about how the building is used?

New regular hirer, a bigger annual event, a room brought back into use, a wall moved, a hall extension. If your busiest realistic occasion has grown past 200 people — counting staff and volunteers — your scope may have changed. Re-run the free checker if you're not sure.

2

Is the named responsible person still the right one?

Churchwardens rotate, clerks move on, trustees stand down. Check the name written in your documents is the person who actually controls the premises today, and that they know the duty sits with them.

Requirement 2 · responsible person · 2 minutes
Checked
3

Are your notification details still accurate?

Once you have notified the regulator, the contact name, email and phone you gave need to stay current. If the person who notified has left, update it.

Requirement 3 · notification · 2 minutes
Checked
4

Walk the four procedures against the actual building

Evacuation: are the exits and assembly point still usable and unobstructed? Invacuation: is the room you named still available, and can it still be secured? Lockdown: do the doors you rely on still lock, and does someone have the key? Communication: is the alert phrase still known, and is the "who calls 999" answer still a real person?

Requirement 4 · the four procedures · 8 minutes, on foot
Checked
5

Do the people on duty still know what to do?

List anyone who has started since the last briefing — new sidespeople, new key-holders, new committee members — and book them in. The Act asks that your staff and volunteers are made aware of the procedures, so keep the briefing records with this sheet.

Requirement 5 · awareness · 3 minutes
Checked
6

Are the hirers and anyone you share with still in the loop?

Check the emergency sheet in your booking pack is the current version, and that any group running the building without you has been given it. If you share the premises, confirm with the other responsible person that your procedures still line up.

Requirement 7 · coordination · 2 minutes
Checked
7

File it and set the next date

Put this sheet in the evidence log with your procedures and briefing records, and note the review in the minutes of your next meeting. Set a reminder for twelve months' time.

Evidence · 1 minute
Checked
Do it sooner than a year if something material changes: a building alteration, a new regular event that is much bigger than anything before, a change of responsible person, or anything that made you think "our plan wouldn't have worked there". A review after a real incident or a near miss is worth more than any diary date.
