Martyn's Law: what this venue has to do

What it is. The Terrorism (Protection of Premises) Act 2025 — "Martyn's Law", named for Martyn Hett, one of the 22 people killed at Manchester Arena in 2017. It asks venues where a good number of people may gather to be prepared to protect them. Duties come into force in Spring 2027, so there is time to do this calmly.

Does it apply to us? One number decides it: could 200 or more people reasonably be present at the same time at our busiest realistic occasion — counting visitors, staff, volunteers and performers? Under 200, the duties do not apply and the sensible action is to record that finding and review it annually. From 200 to 799 we are in the standard tier, the lighter set of duties. Places of worship stay in the standard tier whatever their capacity.

If we are in scope, this is the whole of it

What the law does not ask for

CCTV, barriers, bag searches, security staff, a risk assessment submitted to anyone, a paid consultant, or a course certificate. None of these are standard-tier requirements, and no "compliance certificate" exists in this law. If a provider claims otherwise, ask which of the seven items above it satisfies.

What it would cost us

  • Do it ourselves: £0. The statutory guidance is free on GOV.UK and free awareness training is on ProtectUK. It costs a weekend of someone's time.
  • Template document pack: £149 once. Buys the drafting, not permission — four procedures, grab-card and hirer sheet.
  • Documents and volunteer training: £199 once. Adds the briefing and the records for our evidence log.

What happens if we do nothing

From Spring 2027 the Security Industry Authority can require compliance and issue penalties of up to £10,000 for standard-tier venues, with daily penalties for continuing breaches. The regulator has said its approach will be supportive and proportionate — particularly with venues that have made a start. The more practical risk is the one nobody invoices for: an incident in a building where the people on duty had never been told what to do.

Suggested wording for the minutes

"The meeting considered the requirements of the Terrorism (Protection of Premises) Act 2025. It was noted that the peak number reasonably expected to be present at the busiest occasion is [number], placing the premises [in the standard tier / out of scope]. [Name] was confirmed as the responsible person. It was agreed that the four public protection procedures would be prepared and adopted by [date], that volunteers would be briefed, and that the position would be reviewed annually."
