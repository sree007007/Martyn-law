Four procedures — one decision
GET OUT
Evacuation

Danger is inside → leave, away from it

GET IN
Invacuation

Danger is outside → come in, stay away from glass

KEEP OUT
Lockdown

Attacker approaching → lock, hide, stay quiet

TELL
Communication

Runs through all three → alert, instruct, call 999

The person in charge chooses whichever moves people AWAY from the danger.
Procedure 1 of 4
GET OUT — Evacuation

Used when the threat is inside — for example a suspicious unattended bag.

  • Listen for the announcement: "[your evacuation phrase]".
  • Guide people to the exit away from the danger — that may not be the usual door.
  • Sweep your zone as you go (toilets, kitchen, side rooms) — seconds, not a search.
  • Help those who need it — [named arrangements].
  • Go further than the fire assembly point, out of sight of the building. Don't crowd the doors.
Never touch or investigate a suspicious item. 999 once out.
Procedure 2 of 4
GET IN — Invacuation

Used when the danger is outside — walls between people and danger help.

  • Bring in anyone outside — car park, garden, churchyard, smoking area.
  • Move everyone to our invacuation space: [named area away from glazing and doors].
  • Close external doors; keep people away from windows.
  • Stay calm and keep people together — "this is a precaution".
Be ready to escalate to LOCKDOWN, or switch to EVACUATION if told.
Procedure 3 of 4
KEEP OUT — Lockdown

Used when an attacker is trying to reach people. Short, unmistakable instruction: "LOCKDOWN".

  • Nominated people lock or secure doors — only if it takes seconds and doesn't move you toward the threat.
  • Everyone else: away from doors and glass → into lockable rooms → low → quiet → phones silent.
  • Barricade if a door won't lock.
  • 999 when safe — whisper: where you are, what you saw, how many are with you.
  • Open only for identified police officers.
If escape becomes possible and safe: RUN. HIDE. TELL. — the national rule.
Procedure 4 of 4
TELL — Communication

The procedure that makes the other three work.

  • Our alert method: [announcement / PA / bell / whistle pattern] — one distinct signal per procedure.
  • Scripted first lines are on the grab-card — read them, don't improvise.
  • [Role] calls 999. [Role] meets the police at [point].
  • We warn our neighbours at [premises] — the number is on the grab-card.
Afterwards: only the person in charge gives the all-clear.

Before anything happens: notice and tell
  • Trust your instincts — a person or item that doesn't fit the pattern of our venue is worth a word.
  • Unattended item: don't touch, don't crowd — ask around calmly, then tell the person in charge.
  • Someone testing doors, filming exits, or asking odd questions about busy times — tell the person in charge.
  • Emergency: 999. Non-urgent concern: 101, or the Anti-Terrorist Hotline on 0800 789 321.
  • You will never be criticised for raising something in good faith that turns out to be nothing.
